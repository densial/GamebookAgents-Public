# HOW TO PLAY THIS GAMEBOOK

This is a solo, 5E-compatible adventure. You play one adventurer. The gamebook describes the world, presents choices, tells you when to make checks or saving throws, and provides the rules for any monsters you face.

You do not need a Game Master. When the adventure gives a specific instruction, follow it. When it does not, use the rules in this guide and the abilities on your character sheet.

---

# 1. START PLAYING

## What You Need

You need:

- one ready-to-play character suitable for this adventure;
- a set of roleplaying dice, or a digital dice roller;
- somewhere to record Hit Points, limited-use abilities, spells, conditions, and items you gain or use.

A standard dice set contains:

- `d20` — twenty-sided die;
- `d12` — twelve-sided die;
- `d10` — ten-sided die;
- `d8` — eight-sided die;
- `d6` — six-sided die;
- `d4` — four-sided die.

When the game says `1d20`, roll one twenty-sided die. `2d6` means roll two six-sided dice and add them together. `1d4 + 2` means roll one four-sided die and add 2.

## Choose Your Character

The easiest way to begin is to choose one of the ready-to-play characters provided with the book.

Each character includes the statistics, attacks, equipment, features, and spells needed to play. If a feature can be used only a limited number of times, mark off each use and restore it only when the relevant rule allows you to do so.

If the book allows characters you created yourself, follow the character requirements given for that adventure.

## Begin at the Starting Entry

Gamebook passages are identified by numbered entries.

Start at the entry specified by the adventure. Read that entry and follow its instructions.

You might be told to:

- go directly to another entry;
- choose between two or more actions;
- make an Ability Check;
- make a Saving Throw;
- fight a creature;
- record, use, or lose an item.

When an entry says **Go to 42**, turn to Entry 42 and continue from there.

Do not read other entries to find out what would have happened. Your route through the numbered entries is your adventure.

## Make Your Choices Before Reading Ahead

When the book gives you a choice, decide what your character does before reading the destination entries.

Unless the text specifically allows otherwise, you cannot return to an earlier choice after seeing the result.

Some choices are safer than others. Some reveal different information, lead to different encounters, or change which route you take. A failed roll usually moves the adventure forward rather than asking you to keep rolling until you succeed.

## Your Character Sheet Is Your Record

Keep your character sheet up to date as you play.

Record changes to:

- current Hit Points;
- Temporary Hit Points, if any;
- spell slots and limited-use features;
- conditions;
- ammunition or other resources when the rules require you to track them;
- items gained, consumed, lost, or spent.

Ordinary character state continues until a rule changes it. If you lose Hit Points in one entry, for example, you still have those missing Hit Points when you reach the next entry.

## Specific Instructions Beat General Rules

The adventure sometimes gives special instructions for a particular hazard, creature, or situation.

Follow the specific instruction in the entry or monster description even if the normal rule would be more general.

Examples include:

- a creature that retreats instead of fighting to 0 Hit Points;
- a special way to escape a combat;
- an environmental hazard that deals damage on a failed save;
- an instruction that a failed check cannot be repeated.

If the adventure says **you cannot repeat this check**, accept the result and continue.

# 2. CHECKS AND COMBAT

## The d20 Test

Most uncertain actions use a twenty-sided die.

There are three common kinds of d20 test:

1. **Ability Checks** — attempting something difficult, such as tracking a trail, crossing unstable ground, or investigating a mechanism.
2. **Saving Throws** — resisting or avoiding danger.
3. **Attack Rolls** — trying to hit an enemy.

The basic procedure is:

1. Roll `1d20`.
2. Add the modifier named by the rule or shown on your character sheet.
3. Add your Proficiency Bonus if it applies and is not already included in the listed modifier.
4. Compare the total with the target number.

If your total equals or exceeds the target number, you succeed.

For an Ability Check or Saving Throw, the target is called the **Difficulty Class (DC)**.

For an Attack Roll, the target is the defender's **Armor Class (AC)**.

### Example

The book says:

> Make a DC 12 Wisdom (Survival) check.

Your character has `+4` to Wisdom (Survival).

You roll `9` on the d20.

`9 + 4 = 13`.

Because 13 equals or exceeds DC 12, the check succeeds.

## Ability Checks and Skills

An Ability Check might name only an ability, such as a Strength check, or it might name a skill as well, such as Wisdom (Perception) or Intelligence (Investigation).

Use the modifier shown on your character sheet for the named check.

If the book gives you a choice between checks, choose one before rolling unless the entry says otherwise.

Do not make every listed check and keep the best result.

## Saving Throws

A Saving Throw represents your character resisting a threat.

The book tells you which save to make and what happens on success or failure.

For example:

> Make a DC 11 Strength saving throw.

Use the Strength saving throw modifier on your character sheet, roll the d20, add the modifier, and compare the result with DC 11.

Some characters are proficient in particular Saving Throws. That proficiency is already reflected in a completed character's listed saving throw modifier.

## Advantage and Disadvantage

If you have **Advantage** on a d20 test:

- roll two d20s;
- use the higher roll.

If you have **Disadvantage**:

- roll two d20s;
- use the lower roll.

If Advantage and Disadvantage both apply to the same roll, they cancel each other and you roll one d20.

Unless a rule specifically says otherwise, having more than one source of Advantage does not let you roll additional dice. The same is true of Disadvantage.

## Combat

When combat begins, the entry tells you what creature you are fighting and provides the information needed to run it.

### 1. Roll Initiative

Initiative determines turn order.

Roll `1d20` and add your Initiative modifier. For most characters, this is based on Dexterity and is shown on the character sheet.

For a monster, use its listed Initiative modifier and roll a d20. If its stat block also gives an Initiative score in parentheses, you may use that score instead of rolling for the monster.

Act from the highest Initiative to the lowest.

If you and a monster tie, you are effectively acting as the Game Master for the encounter, so choose which acts first and keep that order for the combat.

### 2. Take Turns

On your turn, you can normally:

- move up to your Speed; and
- take one Action.

You may move before or after your Action, or split your movement around it if nothing prevents you from doing so.

Some features, spells, or items allow a **Bonus Action**. You can take a Bonus Action only when a rule says you can, and you can take no more than one Bonus Action on your turn.

Some abilities allow a **Reaction** when a specific trigger occurs. Follow the ability's instructions when that trigger happens.

## Attacking

Your character sheet lists your attacks and their Attack Roll modifiers.

To make an attack:

1. Roll `1d20`.
2. Add the attack's listed modifier.
3. Compare the total with the target's AC.

If the result equals or exceeds the AC, you hit.

Roll the attack's listed damage and subtract that amount from the target's Hit Points.

### Natural 20 and Natural 1

If the d20 itself shows `20` on an Attack Roll, the attack hits regardless of AC and is a **Critical Hit**.

For a Critical Hit, roll the attack's damage dice twice, add them together, then add the normal modifiers once.

If the d20 itself shows `1` on an Attack Roll, the attack misses regardless of modifiers.

These automatic results apply to Attack Rolls, not ordinary Ability Checks or Saving Throws.

## Running the Monster

A monster's stat block provides its:

- AC;
- Hit Points;
- Initiative;
- Speed;
- attacks;
- special traits and actions.

The encounter may also tell you exactly how the monster behaves.

Follow those instructions rather than making tactical decisions for the monster unless the text leaves a choice unresolved.

If the monster is told to move towards you and attack, do that. If it retreats at a particular Hit Point total, it retreats. If it will not pursue beyond a particular area, it does not pursue you.

Some combats provide an alternative to defeating the enemy, such as forcing a way past it. You may use that option exactly as described.

## Spells and Character Features

Your ready-to-play character explains the spells and special abilities available to you.

If an ability requires an Action, Bonus Action, or Reaction, use it at the appropriate time.

If a spell or feature has limited uses, track those uses.

If it requires a target to make a Saving Throw, use the Save DC shown on your character.

If it requires you to make a Spell Attack, use the Spell Attack modifier shown on your character.

You do not need to memorise the full 5E rules before playing. Read your character's abilities when they become relevant.

# 3. STAYING ALIVE

## Hit Points

Hit Points (HP) measure your ability to keep going.

Your character has:

- a **Hit Point maximum**; and
- **current Hit Points**.

When you take damage, subtract it from your current Hit Points.

Your current Hit Points cannot normally fall below 0 or rise above your Hit Point maximum.

Losing Hit Points does not reduce your attacks, movement, or other capabilities unless a rule specifically says otherwise.

## Healing

When you regain Hit Points, add the amount healed to your current Hit Points, up to your Hit Point maximum.

Healing cannot raise you above your maximum.

Some spells, features, and consumable items restore Hit Points. Follow their descriptions and mark off anything that is consumed or has limited uses.

## Temporary Hit Points

Some abilities may give you Temporary Hit Points.

Temporary Hit Points are lost before your normal Hit Points when you take damage.

They are not healing and are not added to your current Hit Points.

If you receive Temporary Hit Points while you already have some, use either the old amount or the new amount; do not add them together unless a specific rule says otherwise.

Temporary Hit Points do not bring you back to consciousness when you are at 0 Hit Points.

## Dropping to 0 Hit Points

If damage reduces you to 0 Hit Points and does not kill you outright, you become **Unconscious** and begin making Death Saving Throws.

Some encounters give additional instructions for what an enemy does when you fall to 0 Hit Points. Follow those instructions.

### Instant Death from Massive Damage

If damage reduces you to 0 Hit Points and damage remains, compare the remaining damage with your Hit Point maximum.

If the remaining damage equals or exceeds your Hit Point maximum, your character dies immediately.

## Death Saving Throws

At the start of each of your turns while you are at 0 Hit Points and not Stable, roll `1d20`.

- `10` or higher: one success.
- `9` or lower: one failure.
- Natural `1`: two failures.
- Natural `20`: regain 1 Hit Point immediately.

Three successes make you **Stable**.

Three failures mean your character dies.

When you regain any Hit Points or become Stable, erase your accumulated Death Saving Throw successes and failures.

If you take damage while at 0 Hit Points, you normally suffer a Death Saving Throw failure. A Critical Hit causes two failures. Damage large enough to meet the instant-death rule can still kill you immediately.

## Becoming Stable

A Stable character remains at 0 Hit Points and Unconscious but stops making Death Saving Throws.

If a Stable character takes damage, they stop being Stable and begin making Death Saving Throws again.

Unless an encounter gives different instructions, a Stable character who receives no healing regains 1 Hit Point after `1d4` hours.

The gamebook may tell you where to continue after you recover.

## Monster Hit Points

Track a monster's Hit Points during combat.

Normally, a monster is defeated when it reaches 0 Hit Points. Some gamebook creatures instead flee, disperse, surrender, or otherwise end combat before reaching 0.

Always follow the creature's specific encounter rules.

## Conditions

Some hazards, monsters, spells, or character features can impose a **condition**, such as Poisoned or Unconscious.

When a condition is applied, follow the rule provided by the adventure, your character material, or the game's quick-reference material. Keep the condition until the rule that caused it says it ends.

## Equipment and Consumables

If you find an item and the game tells you to record it, add it to your inventory.

If an item is consumed, lost, destroyed, or spent, remove it or mark it as used.

If the text specifically says that an item is **not consumed or damaged**, you keep it.

Do not assume that equipment discovered on another possible route is in your possession. You have only the items your character started with and those you actually obtained during your route through the adventure.

## When Your Character Dies

If your character dies and the adventure gives no special instruction that allows play to continue, that playthrough ends.

You can begin again with the same character, choose another ready-to-play character, or follow any replay instructions provided by the book.
